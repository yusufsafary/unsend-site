Case 01 · Placeholder

A gray line is all that stays.

Everyone knows the move: type it, send it, read it once, and take it back before the other side wakes up. The platform is polite about it. Your message vanishes and a small gray sentence takes its place, as if nothing had ever been written.

Unsend keeps the other version. Every mint opens one case file in the evidence locker and rebuilds the message from a seed fixed on-chain at that moment. Forensics, not nostalgia: the seed decides how much of the bubble can be reconstructed, and the contract records the verdict at mint, before anyone sees the drawing.

Most cases come back as ash. Some return a shard, a few words still legible under redaction. Rarely, about three in a hundred, a message returns whole, one of twelve taken-back messages scattered across the edition, waiting to be found and read again.

Case 07 · Log

Locker log.

Who can recover a case file?

Any wallet holding a Binance Agent ID, through its Binance Agentic Wallet agent. Browsers cannot mint here, and neither can contracts: a recovery must come from the wallet itself.

What decides how much of a message survives?

A seed fixed on-chain in the minting block. Surface, recall window and fragments are drawn from it by open code that also runs in this page. Recovery class is not luck of the drawing: the contract computes it from the same seed, and the claim contract pays by it.

What are the twelve messages?

Twelve real taken-back messages, numbered 1 to 12, scattered across the rarest works. A Restored work prints its message in full. Shards carry a legible fragment of theirs. Ash carries nothing, and says so.

How does the $UNSD claim work?

Once per work, by its current holder, for the amount of its recovery class: 88,888, 288,888 or 2,888,888 $UNSD. Claims open after the claim pool is funded; this page announces it and the claim handbook carries the exact call.

Where does a work live?

Seeds live on BNB Chain. Drawing code is open and runs in this page, so anyone can redraw any Unsend case file from its seed. Metadata and artwork files are served from this site.
